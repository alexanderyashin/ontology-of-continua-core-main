\section{Reader Contract and Navigation Guide}
\label{sec:oc13-reader-contract}

Core~1.3 is the flagship reader-facing monograph, but readers arrive with
different tasks. This guide is therefore a route map rather than a second introduction.
Its job is to show where five reader roles should begin: reviewer, formal
reader, domain reader, broad scientific reader, and practical collaborator.

The references below follow the compiled section and appendix labels of this
flagship build rather than the source filenames used during drafting.

The dedicated figure atlas in
Appendix~\ref{sec:oc-core-1-3-figure-atlas} and the technical-derivation atlas
in Appendix~\ref{sec:oc-core-1-3-technical-derivation-atlas} are intentionally
outside the default continuous reading route. Their material belongs to the
support and reference tier; the formal-reader route below consults the
technical-derivation atlas when local proof support is needed.

\subsection{The five reading routes}

\textbf{Reviewer.}
Read the front matter, this guide,
Sections~\ref{sec:results},
\ref{sec:oc13-publication-boundary}--\ref{sec:oc13-theorem-roadmap},
Section~\ref{sec:oc13-worked-examples},
and then
Appendices~\ref{sec:oc13-source-audit-appendix}--\ref{sec:oc13-reviewer-navigation}. That route exposes the publication boundary, theorem status,
load-bearing proof spine, reviewer objections, and source witnesses as quickly
as possible.

\textbf{Formal reader.}
Use Sections~\ref{sec:intro}--\ref{sec:conclusion} for orientation only.
Then read Sections~\ref{sec:boundary-extended}--\ref{sec:falsifiability-extended}
and Sections~\ref{sec:oc13-publication-boundary}--\ref{sec:oc-core-1-3-toe-synthesis}
in order. Use Appendices~\ref{app:notation}--\ref{app:klevels-tables},
Appendix~\ref{sec:oc13-proof-machinery-appendix}, and the technical-derivation
atlas in Appendix~\ref{sec:oc-core-1-3-technical-derivation-atlas}
when local proof support is needed. This is the compact route that keeps
exact doctrine, theorem scope, and proof machinery visible at once.

\textbf{Domain reader.}
Read Section~\ref{sec:disciplines-extended} and
Sections~\ref{sec:oc13-worked-examples}--\ref{sec:oc-core-1-3-practical-utility},
then move into
Appendices~\ref{sec:oc-core-1-3-empirical-validation-matrix},
\ref{sec:oc-core-1-3-domain-benchmark-manifest},
\ref{sec:oc-core-1-3-domain-benchmark-caseset},
\ref{sec:oc-core-1-3-domain-replay-reports},
\ref{sec:oc-core-1-3-domain-execution-board}, and
\ref{sec:oc-core-1-3-practical-utility-atlas}.
That path exposes bounded domain embedding, worked interpretation, empirical
promotion rules, replay surfaces, benchmark cases, execution board, practical
use, and comparison evidence without forcing the reader to traverse every
technical-derivation section first.

\textbf{Broad scientific reader.}
Read Sections~\ref{sec:intro}--\ref{sec:conclusion},
Sections~\ref{sec:boundary-extended}--\ref{sec:falsifiability-extended}, and
Sections~\ref{sec:oc13-publication-boundary}--\ref{sec:oc-core-1-3-practical-utility}
straight through as a continuous graduate-level monograph. Use
Appendix~\ref{app:notation} for
notation when needed and defer Appendices~\ref{sec:oc-core-1-3-figure-atlas}--\ref{sec:oc-core-1-3-practical-utility-atlas} until a concrete figure,
benchmark table, or support dossier question arises. This is the recommended
route for a broad scientific reader.

\textbf{Practical collaborator.}
Read the front matter, this guide,
Section~\ref{sec:oc13-publication-boundary},
Section~\ref{sec:oc-core-1-3-practical-utility}, and
Appendix~\ref{sec:oc-core-1-3-practical-utility-atlas} first. Return
to Sections~\ref{sec:oc-core-1-3-operationalization-program}--\ref{sec:oc-core-1-3-toe-synthesis}
only when a practical use case needs theorem, replay, or falsifier support to
be inspected in detail. This is the compact route for a practical collaborator
asking what OC now lets the collaborator predict, audit, decide, or compare within declared
bounds.

\subsection{What each major block is for}

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Orientation (Sections~\ref{sec:intro}--\ref{sec:conclusion}).} Problem,
      background, model, results overview, discussion, and conclusion.
\item \textbf{Formal doctrine (Sections~\ref{sec:boundary-extended}--\ref{sec:falsifiability-extended}).} Boundary,
      thresholds, K-level doctrine, operators, collapse and rebirth,
      branching, disciplinary embedding, and falsifiability logic.
\item \textbf{Core~1.3 closure (Sections~\ref{sec:oc13-publication-boundary}--\ref{sec:oc-core-1-3-practical-utility}).}
      Publication boundary, foundational consistency, theorem navigation,
      worked examples, operational and empirical closure discipline, proof
      machinery, Theory of Everything (TOE) synthesis, and practical consequences.
\item \textbf{Reader-facing audit appendices (Appendices~\ref{app:notation}--\ref{sec:oc13-proof-machinery-appendix}).} Notation,
      axiomatic support, K-level tables, parameter and numerical tables,
      source-audit and reviewer matrices, journal-core bridge, empirical
      validation surfaces, institute-run measurement programme, benchmark
      manifest and caseset, replay reports, execution board, and proof-machinery
      appendix.
\item \textbf{Atlas and support appendices (Appendices~\ref{sec:oc-core-1-3-figure-atlas}--\ref{sec:oc-core-1-3-practical-utility-atlas}).} Figure Atlas,
      Technical Derivation Atlas, Reference and Benchmark Atlas, TOE
      Support Dossiers, and Practical Utility and Model Comparison Atlas.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{What this guide is not}

This guide does not restate the theory, replace the theorem roadmap, or turn
the flagship monograph into a dashboard. It simply tells the reader where each
task belongs. Section~\ref{sec:oc13-theorem-roadmap} owns proof navigation.
Section~\ref{sec:oc-core-1-3-toe-synthesis} owns TOE synthesis.
Section~\ref{sec:oc-core-1-3-practical-utility} owns practical consequences
and serious comparison. The appendices keep the inspection burden inside the
same volume without displacing the main reading spine.
