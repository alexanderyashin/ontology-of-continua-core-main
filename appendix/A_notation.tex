\section{Notation and Symbols}
\label{app:notation}

This appendix collects the main symbols and notational conventions used
throughout the Core~1.3 master monograph. It is not exhaustive, but it covers
the structural elements that recur across the main proof-bearing chapters and
the science appendices.

\subsection{Continuum Structure}

\begin{description}
  \item[\(K\)] A continuum (generic level).
  \item[\(K_x\)] Continuum at level \(x\in\{0,\dots,12\}\).
  \item[\(K(t)\)] Time-sliced continuum object obtained from a fixed continuum
        \(K\) at local time \(t\). If a chapter needs the internal state
        separately, it names that state as \(s(t)\in\Omega(K,t)\).
  \item[\(M_x\)] Embedding space for level \(K_x\).
  \item[\(M_x(t)\)] Time-indexed embedding space for \(K_x\) when the
        surrounding chapter allows the environment itself to vary.
  \item[\(\Omega(K)\)] Set of admissible states of \(K\).
  \item[\(\Omega(K,t)\)] Time-indexed admissible states of \(K\) at time
        \(t\); this is the dynamic specialization of \(\Omega(K)\) used when a
        chapter needs the admissible set at a specific instant.
        Legacy expressions such as \(\Omega(K(t))\), \(\Theta(K(t))\), and
        \(C(K(t))\) denote the same time slice as the comma forms
        \(\Omega(K,t)\), \(\Theta(K,t)\), and \(C(K,t)\); the comma form is
        the preferred notation in Core~1.3.
  \item[\(\mathbf{1}_{\Omega\neq\emptyset}(K,t)\)] Nonemptiness indicator for
        the time-slice admissible region: it is \(1\) exactly when
        \(\Omega(K,t)\neq\emptyset\) and \(0\) otherwise.
  \item[\(\Sigma_{\Omega}(K;M_x)\)] Threshold-saturation locus of
        \(\Omega(K)\) inside the declared ambient embedding space \(M_x\):
        \[
          \Sigma_{\Omega}(K;M_x)=
          \{s\in\overline{\Omega(K)}^{\,M_x}\mid
          \exists i\in I_{\mathrm{act}}(K;M_x):\,f_i(s)=0\}.
        \]
        The index set \(I_{\mathrm{act}}(K;M_x)\) contains the declared
        active, nonredundant threshold constraints for the local route.
        Here \(g_i,\Theta_i:\overline{\Omega(K)}^{\,M_x}\to\mathbb{R}\) are
        evaluated on the same local state domain and \(f_i\) is the shifted
        residual form of a raw threshold inequality:
        \(f_i(s)=g_i(s)-\Theta_i(s)\). Thus \(f_i(s)\le0\) is the
        admissible-side convention and \(f_i(s)=0\) is equivalent to the
        pointwise raw saturation equation \(g_i(s)=\Theta_i(s)\). Scalar
        thresholds are the constant-function special case. The same residual
        convention is used in the model and boundary chapters.
  \item[\(\partial\Omega(K)\)] Boundary shorthand for the admissible region
        when the ambient space \(M_x\) and the local regularity convention are
        fixed by the surrounding chapter:
        \[
          \partial\Omega(K)=
          \partial_{M_x}\Omega(K)=\Sigma_{\Omega}(K;M_x).
        \]
        The standing convention is that those active residual thresholds are
        continuous on the ambient closure, the admissible set is the
        intersection of their closed sublevel regions, and no redundant
        saturated constraint is counted as boundary unless the local section
        names it. Sections~\ref{sec:boundary-extended}
        and~\ref{sec:thresholds-extended} fix the boundary and threshold
        assumptions used by this shorthand. Without those ambient and
        regularity hypotheses, use \(\Sigma_{\Omega}(K;M_x)\) and read the
        statement as threshold saturation rather than as a topological
        boundary claim.
  \item[\(\partial\Omega(K,t)\)] Time-indexed admissible boundary of \(K\)
        at time \(t\), equivalently \(\partial_{M_x(t)}\Omega(K,t)\) under
        the threshold family active at that slice when the ambient space varies.
        If the surrounding chapter does not vary the ambient space, the
        shorthand is read relative to the fixed \(M_x\).
  \item[\(A(K)\)] Set of axes of incompatible differences:
        \(A(K) = \{A_1,\dots,A_n\}\).
  \item[\(A(K,t)\)] Time-indexed effective axis set of \(K\), used when the
        working axis family is tracked dynamically.
  \item[\(P(t)\)] Vector of potentials (energetic, chemical, informational,
        biological, cognitive, social, etc.).
  \item[\(J(t)\)] Vector of flows transforming potentials and states.
  \item[\(\Theta(K)\)] Threshold landscape of \(K\), including existence,
        stability, critical, dimensional, death, expressive and embedding
        thresholds.
  \item[\(\Theta(K,t)\)] Time-indexed threshold landscape of \(K\) when the
        admissible boundary is updated during the evolution.
  \item[\(C(K)\)] Family of structurally stable cycles of \(K\).
  \item[\(C(K,t)\)] Time-indexed cycle family of \(K\) when the currently
        surviving stable cycles are tracked dynamically.
  \item[\(k(K,t)\)] Measure of continuumness (viability) of \(K\).
\end{description}

\subsection{Thresholds and Tension}

\begin{description}
  \item[\(\Theta_{\mathrm{exist}}\)] Existence thresholds:
        minimal conditions for \(\Omega(K)\neq\emptyset\).
  \item[\(\Theta_{\mathrm{stab}}\)] Stability thresholds:
        conditions for bounded dynamics.
  \item[\(\Theta_{\mathrm{crit}}\)] Critical thresholds:
        surfaces of qualitative change or phase transition.
  \item[\(\Theta_{\mathrm{dim}}\)] Dimensional thresholds:
        conditions for emergence of new axes.
  \item[\(\Theta_{\mathrm{death}}\)] Death thresholds:
        limits beyond which no admissible states remain.
  \item[\(\Theta_{\mathrm{expr}}\)] Expressive thresholds:
        minimal expressive capacity of axes required to represent relevant
        differences.
  \item[\(\Theta_{\mathrm{embed}}\)] Embedding thresholds:
        constraints imposed by embedding spaces \(M_x\).
  \item[\(T(K,t)\)] Structural tension functional, depending on potentials,
        axes and gradients.
\end{description}

\subsection{Operators}

The time-indexed operators below describe internal dynamics for levels \(K_1\)
and above on time slices of a fixed continuum \(K\). The K0-local glyphs and
cross-level birth operators listed later are separate namespaced entries, not
members of that internal-dynamics family. Level \(K_0\) is non-dynamical and
carries no internal time parameter, so statements about \(K_0\) are expressed
through structural relations rather than through the time-indexed operators
listed here. Where the surrounding level is already fixed, the notation
suppresses that constant \(K\)-dependence only for brevity rather than to imply
that thresholds, cycles, boundaries, axes, or continuumness are level-free.
In those displayed signatures, bare \(M\) abbreviates the active \(M_x\) or
\(M_x(t)\); \(A(t)\), \(\Theta(t)\), \(C(t)\), \(\Omega(t)\), and
\(\partial\Omega(t)\) abbreviate \(A(K,t)\), \(\Theta(K,t)\), \(C(K,t)\),
\(\Omega(K,t)\), and \(\partial\Omega(K,t)\) for the fixed continuum under
discussion.
The displayed signatures in this appendix are reader-facing projections of
the exact operator definitions in Section~\ref{sec:operators-full}. They keep
the principal output component visible while retaining the full coupled
arguments needed by the formal operator chapter.
The K-level archive and the process atlas both use the glyph family
\((\Psi,\Phi,\Lambda,U,\Chi)\), but they do not share one theorem-bearing
namespace. In the K-level archive these glyphs are level-row shorthand; in the
process atlas they are process-layer operators local to the process modules.
The list below is deliberately namespaced: it includes K-level glyphs, dynamic
operators used in the formal chapters, and presentation aliases used only in
specific rows. A glyph entry is therefore not a global definition unless its
entry explicitly says so.
Bare \(\Psi\) in the formal core abbreviates the birth-operator family
\(\{\Psi_{x\to x+1}\}_{x=0}^{11}\) rather than naming a separate process
operator.
The source \(K_0\) archive uses \((\Psi_0,\Phi_0,\Lambda_0)\) as its local
root process glyphs for distinction generation, relational reconfiguration,
and compositional assembly. The TOE synthesis also uses compact aliases such
as \(F_0\), \(Q_0\), and \(U_0\) inside its \(K_0\) numeric rows; those aliases
name local root constraints only and do not inherit the time-indexed \(K_1+\)
dynamic operator meanings listed below.

\begin{description}
  \item[\(E\)] Structural evolution operator:
        \(E: K(t) \to K(t+dt)\).
  \item[\(F\)] Flow operator:
        \(F:(J(t),P(t),A(t),\Theta(t),M)\mapsto J(t+dt)\).
  \item[\(G\)] Potential operator:
        \(G:(P(t),A(t),J(t),\Theta(t),M)\mapsto P(t+dt)\).
  \item[\(H\)] Threshold operator:
        \(H:(\Theta(t),P(t),J(t),M)\mapsto \Theta(t+dt)\).
  \item[\(Q\)] Cycle operator:
        \(Q:(C(t),P(t),J(t),\Theta(t))\mapsto C(t+dt)\).
  \item[\(R\)] Boundary operator:
        \(R:(\partial\Omega(t),P(t),J(t),\Theta(t),M)\mapsto
        \partial\Omega(t+dt)\).
  \item[\(S\)] Structural operator:
        \(S:(A(t),P(t),J(t),\Theta(t),M)\mapsto A(t+dt)\).
  \item[\(U\)] Continuumness operator:
        \(U:(k(t),\Omega(t),A(t),P(t),J(t),\Theta(t),C(t),
        \partial\Omega(t),M)\mapsto k(t+dt)\).
  \item[\(\Psi_0,\Phi_0,\Lambda_0\)] Local \(K_0\) root process glyphs used in
        the source \(K_0\) archive for distinction generation, relational
        reconfiguration, and compositional assembly.
  \item[\(\Phi\)] Meta-space evolution operator used in the K-level archive
        when a level's embedding space is itself allowed to change.
  \item[\(\Lambda\)] Constraint operator enforcing the active law or
        admissibility family in the relevant meta-domain.
  \item[\(\Chi\)] Logical-branching operator used when the formal branch
        structure of a continuum must be tracked explicitly. This is the
        K-level archive convention; process modules that use \(\Chi\) for
        collapse define that use locally.
  \item[\(\Psi_{x\to x+1}\)] Birth operator for \(x\in\{0,\dots,11\}\):
        admissible transition \((K_x,M_x)\mapsto (K_{x+1},M_{x+1})\) when
        the surrounding birth, embedding, and threshold conditions are
        satisfied.
  \item[\(E_{\mathrm{int}}\)] Interaction operator for multiple continua.
\end{description}

\subsection{Levels and Embedding Spaces}

The labels below are mnemonic descriptions for quick notation lookup. The
canonical level doctrine is stated in Section~\ref{sec:klevels-full} and the
generated K-level archive.

\begin{description}
  \item[\(K_0\)] Structural substrate (no time, no geometry, no energy).
  \item[\(K_1\)] One--dimensional classical continua.
  \item[\(K_2\)] Physical continua (fields, phases, percolation,
        Berezinskii--Kosterlitz--Thouless (BKT) transitions,
        mass--related structures).
  \item[\(K_3\)] Chemical continua (reaction networks,
        reflexively autocatalytic and food-generated (RAF) closure).
  \item[\(K_4\)] Protocellular continua (membranes, gradients, osmotic and
        curvature thresholds).
  \item[\(K_5\)] Early neural and bioelectrical continua (ion channels,
        excitability, spikes).
  \item[\(K_6\)] Cognitive continua (representations, binding, prediction).
  \item[\(K_7\)] Social continua (norms, institutions, trust).
  \item[\(K_8\)] Civilizational continua (infrastructures, systemic
        thresholds).
  \item[\(K_9\)] Theory-level continua: theories, paradigms, ontologies, and
        formal languages treated as structured objects.
  \item[\(K_{10}\)] Meta-theoretical continua: model-of-models recursion and
        self-referential theory structure.
  \item[\(K_{11}\)] Evolving meta-theoretical spaces: operator and model-space
        evolution above local meta-model recursion.
  \item[\(K_{12}\)] Global coherence layer: cross-framework compatibility,
        integrability, and the admissible upper envelope of the public
        hierarchy.
\end{description}

\subsection{Miscellaneous Symbols}

\begin{description}
  \item[\(\dim(X)\)] General structural dimension convention for any object
        \(X\) that is already represented as an axis set, structural set, or
        locally linearized space in the surrounding argument.
  \item[\(\dim(K)\)] For \(K_1\) and above, the dimensionality of continuum
        \(K\), fixed in this monograph as \(\dim(K)=|A(K)|\), where \(A(K)\)
        is its effective axis set.
  \item[\(\dim(K(t))\)] Time-slice dimensionality convention for an evolving
        continuum: \(\dim(K(t)) = |A(K,t)|\), where \(A(K,t)\) is the
        effective axis set at that slice.
  \item[\(\dim(\Omega(K))\)] Dimension of the admissible state space in the
        active ambient topology or local linearisation.
  \item[\(\dim(S,\Delta,\mathcal{C})\)] Dimension of a structural tuple, read
        as the dimension of the structural set equipped with its declared
        difference and connectivity data.
  \item[\(\dim(K_0)\)] Base-level dimension convention for the structural
        substrate: \(\dim(K_0)=0\), because \(K_0\) carries
        distinguishability without a geometric axis family of the higher-level
        kind. Abstract \(K_0\) structural variation in \(A(K_0)\) is therefore
        not counted by the higher-level effective-axis dimension \(\dim(K)\).
  \item[\(r_{\mathrm{lin}}(K)\)] Optional local linearized rank notion for a
        section-specific approximation. It is introduced separately whenever a
        local argument needs a rank concept distinct from \(\dim(K)\).
  \item[\(C_{\max}(t)\)] Maximal compatible cycle set at the time slice used by
        the operator chapter's death criterion.
  \item[\(C_{\max}(K)\)] Maximal structurally stable cycle complex or set
        associated with \(K\) under the full threshold landscape
        \(\Theta(K)\). Time-slice death checks use \(C_{\max}(t)\), and
        summary statistics must be named separately.
  \item[\(\mathrm{span}_{\mathrm{str}}(A)\)] Structural span of a set of
        axes: the closure of the admissible structural combinations of those
        axes inside the fixed ambient continuum or meta-space used by the
        local argument.
  \item[\(\mathrm{span}_{\mathrm{lin}}(A)\)] Ordinary linear span of a set of
        axes inside a local linearization. It is used only when a section
        states an explicit linear approximation.
  \item[\(\mathrm{span}(A)\)] Shorthand for
        \(\mathrm{span}_{\mathrm{str}}(A)\) in theorem-bearing structural
        transition, projection, and branching arguments unless a section
        explicitly declares a local linearisation. Use
        \(\mathrm{span}_{\mathrm{lin}}(A)\) only under such an explicit linear
        approximation. The subscripted forms remain preferred.
  \item[\(\chi_{\Omega}(s;K,t)\)] Binary admissibility indicator with codomain
        \(\{0,1\}\): \(\chi_{\Omega}(s;K,t)=1\) exactly when the state \(s\)
        of \(K\) at time \(t\) lies in \(\Omega(K,t)\), and
        \(\chi_{\Omega}(s;K,t)=0\) otherwise. The symbol
        \(\chi_{\Omega}\) is reserved for pointwise membership; use
        \(\mathbf{1}_{\Omega\neq\emptyset}(K,t)\) for nonemptiness of the
        admissible region itself.
\end{description}

This notation appendix is intentionally compact; more specialised symbols used
only in particular extension papers are defined locally in those texts.
