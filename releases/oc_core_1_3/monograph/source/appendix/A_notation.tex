\section{Notation and Symbols}
\label{app:notation}

This appendix collects the main symbols and notational conventions used
throughout the Core~1.3 master monograph. It is not exhaustive, but it covers
the structural elements that recur across the main proof-bearing chapters and
the science appendices.

\subsection{Continuum Structure}

\begin{description}
  \item[\(K\)] A continuum (generic level).
  \item[\(K_x\)] Continuum at level \(x\in\{0,\dots,12\}\).
  \item[\(K(t)\)] Time-sliced continuum object obtained from a fixed continuum
        \(K\) at local time \(t\). If a chapter needs the internal state
        separately, it names that state as \(s(t)\in\Omega(K,t)\).
  \item[\(\tau(K)\)] Scalar level-time symbol used by the K-level chapters when
        a level has an effective temporal rhythm. In those chapters
        \(\tau(K_x)\) is the local scalar alias of
        \(\tau_{\mathrm{eff}}(K_x)\). A finite ordered family of temporal
        parameters must be written with a decorated family symbol such as
        \(\boldsymbol{\tau}(K)=(\tau_1(K),\dots,\tau_m(K))\). If the level is
        atemporal or the local construction does not represent time,
        \(\tau(K)\) is left undefined rather than silently replaced by a trivial
        time object. An actual temporal order relation, when needed, is written
        separately, for example as \(\preceq_K\), and is not denoted by
        \(\tau(K)\). Topology is written as a paired structure such as
        \((X_1,\tau_1)\), or as the legacy local alias \((X,\tau)\) when the
        local \(K_1\) display binds it explicitly.
  \item[\(\tau_{\mathrm{eff}}(K)\)] Scalar effective time or rhythm selected
        from the temporal-parameter family \(\boldsymbol{\tau}(K)\) by the local rule
        declared in the surrounding chapter. When \(\tau_{C_j}\) is a cycle
        period and the selected rhythm is the slowest non-degradable cycle, the
        corresponding extremum is \(\max_j\tau_{C_j}\). Displays using
        \(\min_j\tau_{C_j}\) name the fastest closed rhythm and must not be read
        as the slowest-cycle convention.
  \item[\(M_x\)] Embedding space for level \(K_x\).
  \item[\(M_x(t)\)] Time-indexed embedding space for \(K_x\) when the
        surrounding chapter allows the environment itself to vary.
  \item[\(\Omega(K)\)] Set of admissible states of \(K\).
  \item[\(\Omega(K,t)\)] Time-indexed admissible states of \(K\) at time
        \(t\); this is the dynamic specialization of \(\Omega(K)\) used when a
        chapter needs the admissible set at a specific instant.
        Legacy expressions such as \(\Omega(K(t))\) and \(C(K(t))\)
        denote the same time slice as the comma forms \(\Omega(K,t)\) and
        \(C(K,t)\); the comma form is the preferred notation in Core~1.3.
  \item[\(\Omega_{\mathrm{law}}(K)\)] Law-level admissibility carrier for
        \(K\), used when the rules defining admissible states must be separated
        from the live states currently occupied.
  \item[\(\Omega_t(K)\)] Compact bridge notation for the time-slice
        \(\Omega(K,t)\).
  \item[\(\Omega_{\mathrm{res}}(K,t)\)] Residual structural support that may
        remain at time \(t\) after live admissibility for \(K\) has vanished.
  \item[\(\mathbf{1}_{\{\Omega(K,t)\neq\emptyset\}}\)] Nonemptiness indicator for
        the time-slice admissible region: it is \(1\) exactly when
        \(\Omega(K,t)\neq\emptyset\) and \(0\) otherwise.
  \item[\(H(\Omega(K_x))\)] Legacy K-level notation for the same nonemptiness
        factor in continuity products. It is a Boolean admissibility indicator,
        not the threshold-evolution operator \(H_{\mathrm{thr}}\).
  \item[\(\Sigma_{\Omega}(K_x;M_x)\)] Threshold-saturation locus of
        \(\Omega(K_x)\) inside the declared ambient embedding space \(M_x\):
        \[
          \Sigma_{\Omega}(K_x;M_x)=
          \{s\in\overline{\Omega(K_x)}^{\,M_x}\mid
          \exists i\in I_{\mathrm{act}}(K_x;M_x):\,f_i(s)=0\}.
        \]
        Here \(g_i,\theta_i^{\mathrm{bdry}}:
        \overline{\Omega(K_x)}^{\,M_x}\to\mathbb{R}\) are evaluated on the same
        local state domain and \(f_i\) is the shifted residual form of a raw
        threshold inequality:
        \(f_i(s)=g_i(s)-\theta_i^{\mathrm{bdry}}(s)\). Thus \(f_i(s)\le0\) is
        the admissible-side convention and \(f_i(s)=0\) is equivalent to the
        pointwise raw saturation equation \(g_i(s)=
        \theta_i^{\mathrm{bdry}}(s)\). The decorated
        \(\theta_i^{\mathrm{bdry}}\) is the local boundary-threshold function,
        not the global threshold family \(\Theta(K)\). Scalar thresholds are
        the constant-function special case. Let
        \(I_{\Theta}(K_x;M_x)\) be the finite threshold family declared for this
        local boundary computation. The active set is
        \[
          I_{\mathrm{act}}(K_x;M_x)=
          \left\{i\in I_{\Theta}(K_x;M_x)\,\middle|\,
          \begin{array}{l}
          \exists s\in\overline{\Omega(K_x)}^{\,M_x}: f_i(s)=0,\\
          \text{and every neighbourhood }U\ni s\text{ in }
          \overline{\Omega(K_x)}^{\,M_x}\text{ contains a point }u\in U\\
          \text{with } f_j(u)\le0\text{ for all }j\in I_{\Theta}(K_x;M_x)\setminus\{i\}\text{ but }f_i(u)>0
          \end{array}\right\}.
        \]
        Thus \(I_{\mathrm{act}}\) records constraints whose equality can be
        reached and whose removal has a local violating witness. The stronger
        reading that removing the constraint enlarges the full admissible side
        requires the regularity and constraint-qualification assumptions stated
        locally; without those assumptions, \(I_{\mathrm{act}}\) is used only as
        a threshold-saturation witness. The same residual convention is used in
        the model and boundary chapters.
  \item[\(\partial\Omega(K_x)\)] Boundary of the admissible states of \(K_x\).
        In theorem-bearing threshold contexts this is the threshold-defined
        admissible boundary recorded by the active saturation locus
        \(\Sigma_{\Omega}(K_x;M_x)\). The ambient topological boundary
        \(\partial_{M_x}^{\mathrm{top}}\Omega(K_x)\) is a separate derived
        topological object. A local section may identify
        \(\partial\Omega(K_x)\) with
        \(\partial_{M_x}^{\mathrm{top}}\Omega(K_x)\) only after it states the
        required regularity and constraint-qualification assumptions.
  \item[\(\partial\Omega(K_x,t)\)] Time-indexed admissible boundary of \(K_x\)
        at time \(t\), equivalently \(\partial_{M_x(t)}\Omega(K_x,t)\) under
        the threshold family active at that slice when the ambient space
        varies. If the surrounding chapter does not vary the ambient space, the
        shorthand is read relative to the fixed \(M_x\).
  \item[\(A(K)\)] Set of axes of incompatible differences:
        \(A(K) = \{A_1,\dots,A_n\}\).
  \item[\(A(K,t)\)] Time-indexed effective axis set of \(K\), used when the
        working axis family is tracked dynamically.
  \item[\(P(t)\)] Vector of potentials (energetic, chemical, informational,
        biological, cognitive, social, etc.).
  \item[\(J(t)\)] Vector of flows transforming potentials and states.
  \item[\(\Theta(K)\)] Threshold landscape of \(K\), including existence,
        stability, critical, dimensional, death, expressive and embedding
        thresholds.
  \item[\(\Theta(K,t)\)] Time-indexed threshold landscape of \(K\) when the
        admissible boundary is updated during the evolution.
  \item[\(C(K)\)] Family of structurally stable cycles of \(K\).
  \item[\(C(K,t)\)] Time-indexed cycle family of \(K\) when the currently
        surviving stable cycles are tracked dynamically.
  \item[\(k(K,t)\)] Measure of continuumness (viability) of \(K\).
\end{description}

\subsection{Thresholds and Tension}

\begin{description}
  \item[\(\Theta_{\mathrm{exist}}\)] Existence thresholds:
        minimal conditions for \(\Omega(K)\neq\emptyset\).
  \item[\(\Theta_{\mathrm{stab}}\)] Stability thresholds:
        conditions for bounded dynamics.
  \item[\(\Theta_{\mathrm{crit}}\)] Critical thresholds:
        surfaces of qualitative change or phase transition.
  \item[\(\Theta_{\mathrm{dim}}\)] Dimensional thresholds:
        conditions for emergence of new axes.
  \item[\(\Theta_{\mathrm{time}}\)] Temporal-stability threshold used in
        K-level time-emergence clauses. A cycle contributes to scalar level-time
        only when its persistence or closure score satisfies the local
        \(\Pi(C_j)>\Theta_{\mathrm{time}}\) obligation.
  \item[\(\Pi(C_j)\)] Local persistence or closure score assigned to a cycle
        \(C_j\) when the time-emergence clause requires a scalar witness.
  \item[\(\Theta_{\mathrm{death}}\)] Death thresholds:
        limits beyond which no admissible states remain.
  \item[\(\Theta_{\mathrm{expr}}\)] Expressive thresholds:
        minimal expressive capacity of axes required to represent relevant
        differences.
  \item[\(\Theta_{\mathrm{embed}}\)] Embedding thresholds:
        constraints imposed by embedding spaces \(M_x\).
  \item[\(T(K,t)\)] Structural tension functional, depending on potentials,
        axes and gradients.
\end{description}

\subsection{Operators}

The time-indexed operators below describe internal dynamics for levels \(K_1\)
and above on time slices of a fixed continuum \(K\). The K0-local glyphs and
cross-level birth operators listed later are separate namespaced entries, not
members of that internal-dynamics family. Level \(K_0\) is non-dynamical and
carries no internal time parameter, so statements about \(K_0\) are expressed
through structural relations rather than through the time-indexed operators
listed here. Where the surrounding level is already fixed, the notation
suppresses that constant \(K\)-dependence only for brevity rather than to imply
that thresholds, cycles, boundaries, axes, or continuumness are level-free.
In those displayed signatures, bare \(M\) abbreviates the active \(M_x\) or
\(M_x(t)\); \(A(t)\), \(\Theta(t)\), \(C(t)\), \(\Omega(t)\), and
\(\partial\Omega(t)\) abbreviate \(A(K,t)\), \(\Theta(K,t)\), \(C(K,t)\),
\(\Omega(K,t)\), and \(\partial\Omega(K,t)\) for the fixed continuum under
discussion.
The displayed signatures in this appendix are reader-facing projections of
the exact operator definitions in Section~\ref{sec:operators-full}. They keep
the principal output component visible while retaining the full coupled
arguments needed by the formal operator chapter.
The K-level archive and the process atlas both use the glyph family
\((\Psi,\Phi,\Lambda,U,\Chi)\), but they do not share one theorem-bearing
namespace. In the K-level archive these glyphs are level-row shorthand; in the
process atlas they are process-layer operators local to the process modules.
The list below is deliberately namespaced: it includes K-level glyphs, dynamic
operators used in the formal chapters, and presentation aliases used only in
specific rows. A glyph entry is therefore not a global definition unless its
entry explicitly says so.
Bare \(\Psi\) denotes the generic birth/rebirth operator family of the formal
core. Its indexed forms state the route being used: \(\Psi_{x\to x+1}\)
denotes adjacent-level birth, while a rebirth route may use a same-level or
cross-level candidate \(K'\) as specified by the local rebirth predicate.
Legacy or local process uses must carry their level or module subscript.
The source \(K_0\) archive uses \((\Psi_0,\Phi_0,\Lambda_0)\) as its local
root process glyphs for distinction generation, relational reconfiguration,
and compositional assembly. The unified synthesis also uses compact aliases such
as \(F_0\), \(Q_0\), and \(U_0\) inside its \(K_0\) numeric rows; those aliases
name local root constraints only and do not inherit the time-indexed \(K_1+\)
dynamic operator meanings listed below.

\begin{description}
  \item[\(E_{\mathrm{evol}}\)] Structural evolution operator, often written
        locally as \(E\) after the chapter has bound the symbol:
        \(E_{\mathrm{evol}}: K(t) \to K(t+dt)\). The decorated form avoids
        collision with energy quantities such as \(E(K_x)\).
  \item[\(F\)] Flow operator:
        \(F:(J(t),P(t),A(t),\Theta(t),M)\mapsto J(t+dt)\).
  \item[\(G\)] Potential operator:
        \(G:(P(t),A(t),J(t),\Theta(t),M)\mapsto P(t+dt)\).
  \item[\(H_{\mathrm{thr}}\)] Threshold-evolution operator, often written
        locally as \(H\) after the chapter has bound the symbol:
        \(H_{\mathrm{thr}}:(\Theta(t),P(t),J(t),M)\mapsto \Theta(t+dt)\).
  \item[\(Q\)] Cycle operator:
        \(Q:(C(t),P(t),J(t),\Theta(t))\mapsto C(t+dt)\).
  \item[\(R\)] Boundary operator:
        \(R:(\partial\Omega(t),P(t),J(t),\Theta(t),M)\mapsto
        \partial\Omega(t+dt)\).
  \item[\(S_{\mathrm{axis}}\)] Axis-structure operator, often written locally
        as \(S\) after the chapter has bound the symbol:
        \(S_{\mathrm{axis}}:(A(t),P(t),J(t),\Theta(t),M)\mapsto A(t+dt)\).
        The decorated form avoids collision with scalar complexity \(S(K)\).
  \item[\(U\)] Continuumness operator:
        \(U:(k(t),\Omega(t),A(t),P(t),J(t),\Theta(t),C(t),
        \partial\Omega(t),M)\mapsto k(t+dt)\).
  \item[\(\mathrm{DoF}(X)\)] Degrees of freedom available to the continuum,
        level, or embedding object \(X\) in the local compatibility test.
        The K0 synthesis rows use \(\mathrm{DoF}(K_x)/\mathrm{DoF}(M_x)\)
        as a dimensionless meta-space compatibility ratio only on rows where
        \(\mathrm{DoF}(M_x)>0\); if the embedding denominator is zero, the row
        must state compatibility explicitly rather than using the quotient.
  \item[\(C_{\mathrm{nt}}\)] Non-trivial lawful-cycle presence count or
        indicator in the local synthesis row. The threshold
        \(C_{\mathrm{nt}}\geq 1\) means that at least one non-trivial lawful
        cycle remains available to the tested structure.
  \item[\(\Psi_0,\Phi_0,\Lambda_0\)] Local \(K_0\) root process glyphs used in
        the source \(K_0\) archive for distinction generation, relational
        reconfiguration, and compositional assembly.
  \item[\(\Phi\)] Meta-space evolution operator used in the K-level archive
        when a level's embedding space is itself allowed to change.
  \item[\(\Lambda\)] Constraint operator enforcing the active law or
        admissibility family in the relevant meta-domain.
  \item[\(\Chi\)] Logical-branching operator used when the formal branch
        structure of a continuum must be tracked explicitly. This is the
        K-level archive convention; process modules that use \(\Chi\) for
        collapse define that use locally.
  \item[\(\Psi_{x\to x+1}\)] Birth operator for \(x\in\{0,\dots,11\}\):
        admissible transition \((K_x,M_x)\mapsto (K_{x+1},M_{x+1})\) when
        the surrounding birth, embedding, and threshold conditions are
        satisfied.
  \item[\(\Psi_{x\to y}\)] Non-adjacent birth-route notation for \(y>x\). It is
        not an additional primitive unless a local theorem explicitly declares
        it so; by default it abbreviates the admissible composition
        \(\Psi_{y-1\to y}\circ\cdots\circ\Psi_{x\to x+1}\), with every
        intermediate threshold and embedding obligation still required.
  \item[\(\Psi_{\mathrm{reb}}\)] Rebirth-route operator family. A candidate-
        specific use is written
        \(\Psi_{\mathrm{reb}}(K,K',h;R_{\mathrm{res}}(K),\mathcal{M},\mathcal{M}')\):
        \(R_{\mathrm{res}}(K)\) is the residue set of the collapsed continuum,
        \(\mathcal{M}\) and \(\mathcal{M}'\) are the source and target
        embedding spaces, \(h:\mathcal{M}\to\mathcal{M}'\) is the witness map,
        and the later candidate \(K'\) is tested against residue, threshold
        conditions, and identity conditions. This notation allows same-level
        rebirth as well as cross-level rebirth when the local Definition~12.6
        route permits it.
  \item[\(E_{\mathrm{int}}\)] Interaction operator for multiple continua.
\end{description}

\subsection{Levels and Embedding Spaces}

The labels below are mnemonic descriptions for quick notation lookup. The
canonical level doctrine is stated in Section~\ref{sec:klevels-full} and the
generated K-level archive.

\begin{description}
  \item[\(K_0\)] Structural substrate (no time, no geometry, no energy).
  \item[\(K_1\)] One--dimensional classical continua.
  \item[\(K_2\)] Physical continua (fields, phases, percolation,
        Berezinskii--Kosterlitz--Thouless (BKT) transitions,
        mass--related structures).
  \item[\(K_3\)] Chemical continua (reaction networks,
        reflexively autocatalytic and food-generated (RAF) closure).
  \item[\(K_4\)] Protocellular continua (membranes, gradients, osmotic and
        curvature thresholds).
  \item[\(K_5\)] Early neural and bioelectrical continua (ion channels,
        excitability, spikes).
  \item[\(K_6\)] Cognitive continua (representations, binding, prediction).
  \item[\(K_7\)] Social continua (norms, institutions, trust).
  \item[\(K_8\)] Civilizational continua (infrastructures, systemic
        thresholds).
  \item[\(K_9\)] Theory-level continua: theories, paradigms, ontologies, and
        formal languages treated as structured objects.
  \item[\(K_{10}\)] Meta-theoretical continua: model-of-models recursion and
        self-referential theory structure.
  \item[\(K_{11}\)] Evolving meta-theoretical spaces: operator and model-space
        evolution above local meta-model recursion.
  \item[\(K_{12}\)] Global coherence layer: cross-framework compatibility,
        integrability, and the admissible upper envelope of the public
        hierarchy.
\end{description}

\subsection{Miscellaneous Symbols}

\begin{description}
  \item[\(\dim(X)\)] General structural dimension convention for any object
        \(X\) that is already represented as an axis set, structural set, or
        locally linearized space in the surrounding argument.
  \item[\(\dim(K)\)] For \(K_1\) and above, the dimensionality of continuum
        \(K\), fixed in this monograph as \(\dim(K)=|A(K)|\), where \(A(K)\)
        is its effective axis set.
  \item[\(\dim(K(t))\)] Time-slice dimensionality convention for an evolving
        continuum: \(\dim(K(t)) = |A(K,t)|\), where \(A(K,t)\) is the
        effective axis set at that slice.
  \item[\(\dim_{\mathrm{cov}}(\Omega(K))\)] Covering dimension of
        \(\Omega(K)\) with the subspace topology inherited from the declared
        ambient space. This notation is used only for nonempty admissible
        regions unless a local empty-set convention is explicitly declared.
  \item[\(\dim_{\mathrm{eff}}(\Omega(K))\)] Effective admissible-state
        dimension used in cross-level emergence comparisons. It may coincide
        with \(\dim_{\mathrm{cov}}(\Omega(K))\) only under the local hypotheses
        stated in the surrounding argument; otherwise it is a rank or active-axis
        proxy, not the literal covering dimension. Legacy displays of
        \(\dim(\Omega(K_1))<\dim(\Omega(K_2))\) in transition schematics are to
        be read as \(\dim_{\mathrm{eff}}\)-comparisons. Local linearized
        approximations may also use a separate symbol such as
        \(r_{\mathrm{lin}}(K)\).
  \item[\(\dim(S,\Delta,\mathcal{C})\)] Dimension of a structural tuple, read
        as the dimension of the structural set equipped with its declared
        difference and connectivity data.
  \item[\(\dim(K_0)\)] Base-level dimension convention for the structural
        substrate: \(\dim(K_0)=0\), because \(K_0\) carries
        distinguishability without a geometric axis family of the higher-level
        kind. Abstract \(K_0\) structural variation in \(A(K_0)\) is therefore
        not counted by the higher-level effective-axis dimension \(\dim(K)\).
  \item[\(r_{\mathrm{lin}}(K)\)] Optional local linearized rank notion for a
        section-specific approximation. It is introduced separately whenever a
        local argument needs a rank concept distinct from \(\dim(K)\).
  \item[\(C_{\max}(t)\)] Maximal compatible cycle set at the time slice used by
        the operator chapter's death criterion.
  \item[\(C_{\max}(K)\)] Maximal structurally stable cycle complex or set
        associated with \(K\) under the full threshold landscape
        \(\Theta(K)\). Time-slice death checks use \(C_{\max}(t)\), and
        summary statistics must be named separately.
  \item[\(\mathrm{span}_{\mathrm{str}}(A)\)] Structural span of a set of
        axes: the smallest structurally closed subobject, inside the fixed
        ambient continuum or meta-space, that contains \(A\) and is closed
        under the locally declared admissible composition, restriction, and
        threshold-respecting transport operations. Equivalently it is the
        intersection of all locally declared structurally closed subobjects
        that contain \(A\). This notation is used only in contexts where that
        local closed-subobject family is nonempty and stable under arbitrary
        intersections; otherwise the section must declare a replacement span
        operation.
  \item[\(\mathrm{span}_{\mathrm{lin}}(A)\)] Ordinary linear span of a set of
        axes inside a local linearization. It is used only when a section
        states an explicit linear approximation.
  \item[\(\mathrm{span}(A)\)] Shorthand for
        \(\mathrm{span}_{\mathrm{str}}(A)\) in theorem-bearing structural
        text unless a section explicitly declares a local linearisation. Use
        \(\mathrm{span}_{\mathrm{lin}}(A)\) only under such an explicit linear
        approximation. The subscripted forms remain preferred.
  \item[\(\chi_{\Omega}(s;K,t)\)] Binary admissibility indicator with codomain
        \(\{0,1\}\): \(\chi_{\Omega}(s;K,t)=1\) exactly when the state \(s\)
        of \(K\) at time \(t\) lies in \(\Omega(K,t)\), and
        \(\chi_{\Omega}(s;K,t)=0\) otherwise. The symbol
        \(\chi_{\Omega}\) is reserved for pointwise membership; use
        \(\mathbf{1}_{\{\Omega(K,t)\neq\emptyset\}}\) for nonemptiness of the
        admissible region itself.
\end{description}

This notation appendix is intentionally compact; more specialised symbols used
only in particular extension papers are defined locally in those texts.

\subsection{OC Core 1.3.3 notation bridge}
\label{sec:oc133-notation-scientific-closure-bridge}

This bridge records the additional notation needed by the current public
interface: the continuum carrier is typed separately from live realizations,
global \(K_0\) discreteness is read through resolution-relative quotient
distinguishability, boundaries act as admissibility classifiers, and
\(F,G,H,Q,R,S,U\) are hybrid update components rather than universal
derivatives.  The published tuple
\[
K=(\Omega,A,P,J,\Theta,\partial\Omega,C,k)
\]
is preserved as a compact interface for
\[
K=\big(\Omega(K),A(K),P(K,t),J(K,t),\Theta(K),
       \partial\Omega(K,t),C(K,t),k(K,t)\big)
\]
at the fixed continuum and instant under discussion.  Thus \(A\) abbreviates
\(A(K)\), \(P\) abbreviates \(P(K,t)\), \(J\) abbreviates \(J(K,t)\),
\(\Theta\) abbreviates \(\Theta(K)\), \(\partial\Omega\) abbreviates
\(\partial\Omega(K,t)\), and \(C\) abbreviates \(C(K,t)\) unless a formula
states a different specialization.  The update component \(H\) is the declared
nonemptiness/status test \(H(\Omega(K_x))\) or its time-slice analogue, not a
new scalar.  Theorem-bearing statements now distinguish
\(\Omega_{\mathrm{law}}(K)\), \(\Omega_t(K)\), and
\(\Omega_{\mathrm{res}}(K,t)\).  A carrier may persist while live admissibility
is empty, so the notation separates carrier persistence from live identity.
Here \(\Omega_{\mathrm{law}}(K)\) denotes the law-level admissibility carrier
for \(K\), \(\Omega_t(K)\) is the time-slice alias for \(\Omega(K,t)\), and
\(\Omega_{\mathrm{res}}(K,t)\) denotes the residual structural support that may
remain after live admissibility has vanished.  In this bridge paragraph, bare
\(k\) abbreviates the already defined diagnostic scalar \(k(K,t)\) for the
continuum and instant under discussion.

Continuumness \(k\) is a diagnostic scalar governed by explicit zero-cause
families.  Live status is a predicate, collapse cause is typed, and the old
multiplicative formula is retained only as a lawful local aggregator under its
declared assumptions.
