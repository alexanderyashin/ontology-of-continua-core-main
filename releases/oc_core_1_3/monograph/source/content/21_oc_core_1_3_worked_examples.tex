\section{Worked Examples, Counterexamples, and Reader-Oriented Interpretive Checks}
\label{sec:oc13-worked-examples}

This chapter turns the formal grammar into bounded reading moves. Its purpose
is to show what the theorem route licenses, what it forbids, and what would
count as a real counterexample at the release boundary. It does not replace the
proof route with storytelling; it tests whether the reader can apply the proof
route without hand-waving. The examples therefore show how the formal grammar
should be read, why the K-level doctrine is not an arbitrary count, and how the
master volume disciplines the shorter journal-core extraction.
In these examples, ``declared'' means named inside the local example data or in
a cited support route before the classification or reduction test is applied;
it never means supplied afterward to rescue a desired reading.

\subsection{Worked Example 1: collapse, residue, and rebirth in a minimal admissible-state model}

Consider a continuum \(K\) whose admissible realization at time \(t\) is described by
\[
\Omega(K,t) = \{x \in [0,1] \mid \phi(x,t) \le \Theta(t)\},
\]
where \(\phi(x,t)\) is an incompatibility load and \(\Theta(t)\) is the currently lawful
threshold. Suppose that at an earlier stage \(t_0\) the set is nonempty, for example because
\(\phi(x,t_0)\) is below threshold on a compact interval \([a,b]\subseteq [0,1]\).

Now assume that the embedding regime changes, the threshold tightens, or the internal evolution
operator drives the incompatibility load upward so that at time \(t_1\),
\[
\forall x \in [0,1] \qquad \phi(x,t_1) > \Theta(t_1).
\]
Then \(\Omega(K,t_1)=\emptyset\). In the language of the source-native death condition, that is
not merely a bad regime or a temporarily unproductive phase. Because the
existence indicator \(\chi_{\Omega}(s_K(t_1);K,t_1)\) is then zero, the
continuumness measure also collapses to \(k(K,t_1)=0\) under the unified
Core~1.3 definition. The implication is immediate because \(k\) is defined as
the product whose first factor is the existence indicator. It is therefore
the lawful death of the continuum under the current admissibility regime.

The important interpretive point is what follows next. Suppose that after collapse, the embedding
space still preserves some structured trace: constraints, marks, gradients, memory residues, or
other inherited structure that a later continuum can exploit. That trace is not the original live
continuum, because the admissible realization has already become empty. It is residue: a surviving
structural aftermath without live identity.

Finally suppose that at time \(t_2>t_1\), after changes in thresholds, embedding, or generative
conditions, a new nonempty admissible realization appears for some later object \(K'\). The
lawful classification is not that the original continuum has revived. The irreversibility rule
forbids that reading. The correct classification is rebirth only when the full predicate of
Definition~12.6 is met: the later live continuum appears in the same or a related
embedding space, inherits at least one structural element from residue, and satisfies the relevant
birth conditions. The separate identity rule in Section~\ref{sec:collapse-rebirth}
then says why that later live continuum is numerically new rather than a
restoration of the original.
If a route uses the related-embedding option rather than the same embedding
space, the relation must be tested against the residue and threshold clauses
rather than inferred after the fact to save a desired rebirth reading. In this
article-level use, one auditable compatibility witness for ``related'' is the explicitly
scoped condition
\[
  \begin{aligned}
  &\exists h:\mathcal{M}\to\mathcal{M}'\ \exists r\in R_{\mathrm{res}}(K)\\
  &\qquad h(r)\in\Omega(K')\subseteq\Omega(\mathcal{M}')\ \text{and}\
  \operatorname{Pres}_{\Theta,\Omega}(h,r;K,K').
  \end{aligned}
\]
Here \(\mathcal{M}\) is the embedding space of the collapsed continuum,
\(\mathcal{M}'\) is the embedding space proposed for the later continuum,
\(R_{\mathrm{res}}(K)\) is the residue set of the collapsed continuum, and
\(\operatorname{Pres}_{\Theta,\Omega}\) means that the named map \(h\)
preserves the residue-bearing substructure and the threshold/admissibility
predicates used to test the later live continuum. This witness is not by
itself the full rebirth predicate; it supplies the related-embedding clause
inside that predicate. This is the
example-specific relation denoted by
\(\mathcal{M}\sim_{\mathrm{res}}\mathcal{M}'\). The formal rebirth predicate
remains Definition~12.6; the source predicate may admit other properly
declared
related-space witnesses; this worked example uses the stricter
\(\sim_{\mathrm{res}}\) witness so the related branch is auditable rather than
assumed.
For this example, the predicate is explicitly:
\[
\operatorname{Pres}_{\Theta,\Omega}(h,r;K,K')
\Longleftrightarrow
\begin{gathered}
h \text{ preserves the residue-bearing substructure containing } r,\\
h(r)\in\Omega(K')\subseteq\Omega(\mathcal{M}'),\ \text{and the threshold/admissibility tests}\\
\mathcal{D}_{\Theta,\Omega}(K';h(r))
=h_{\ast}\mathcal{D}^{\mathrm{res}}_{\Theta,\Omega}(K;r).
\end{gathered}
\]
Here \(\mathcal{D}_{\Theta,\Omega}\) denotes the local admissibility data:
the nonemptiness indicator together with the active residual-threshold signs
and saturated constraints. The equality therefore says that the named map
transports the residue-bearing threshold and admissibility data used to test
the later live continuum; it is not an informal resemblance clause. The
pushforward \(h_{\ast}\) is componentwise: it applies \(h\) to the residue
state, carries each named residual threshold \(f_i\) to the corresponding
declared residual threshold \(f_i'\), preserves the sign relation
\(\le 0\), and preserves the saturated subset \(f_i=0\).

This example is deliberately modest. It does not claim that every physically, biologically, or
socially interesting regime can already be reduced to such a scalar picture. What it does show is
how the theorem grammar should be read once the support chain is accepted. A hostile reviewer may
reject the premises or the admissibility logic. What the reviewer should not be allowed to say is
that the release leaves the post-collapse identity consequences undefined.

\subsection{Worked Example 2: why \texorpdfstring{$K_{11}$}{K11} and \texorpdfstring{$K_{12}$}{K12} are not decorative}

One of the easiest ways to make a foundational framework look more sophisticated than it is would
be to keep extending its hierarchy labels without necessity. Core 1.3 blocks that temptation by
forcing the reader to ask what would break if the hierarchy stopped at \(K_{10}\).

At the level of \(K_9\), the theory already treats theories, paradigms, and ontological schemes as
structured objects. At \(K_{10}\), it treats model-of-models recursion and self-reference as part
of the formal landscape. That still leaves two scientific problems open.

\begin{enumerate}
\item How do operator families, meta-logics, and model-spaces themselves evolve as structured
      continua rather than as static meta-language?
\item How are global coherence conditions expressed once multiple such higher-order objects coexist
      and partially constrain one another?
\end{enumerate}

Stopping at \(K_{10}\) would force those problems back into informal commentary. The public source
corpus, however, already contains the beginnings of explicit treatment for both. That is why
\(K_{11}\) and \(K_{12}\) are retained: not because bigger counts sound grander, but because the
framework has already crossed the line at which the evolution of theory-space and the coherence of
higher-order structures have to be represented formally.

The reader-oriented reduction check here is explicit and bounded. It is an
audit heuristic for seeing what a \(K_{10}\)-only reduction would have to
preserve, not a replacement for the source-owned irreducibility dossier. In this paragraph,
\(\mathrm{Car}(K_n)\) denotes the carrier set of objects assigned to level \(K_n\), not the
level label itself. Let
\(\mathcal{U}_{11,12}=\mathrm{Car}(K_{11})\sqcup \mathrm{Car}(K_{12})\) denote the disjoint union of
objects assigned to the two tested upper-level roles. A \(K_{10}\)-only
reduction is a tuple \((\rho,\equiv_{\rho},\rho_{\ast})\), where
\(\rho:\mathcal{U}_{11,12}\to \mathrm{Car}(K_{10})\),
\(\equiv_{\rho}\subseteq\mathcal{U}_{11,12}\times\mathcal{U}_{11,12}\) is the
declared compatibility relation induced by the translation, and
\(\rho_{\ast}\) is shorthand for the family of transports
\(\{\rho_{\ast,X}\}_X\) that sends upper-level requirements to their proposed
\(K_{10}\) counterparts. For each object \(X\), let
\(\mathcal{B}(X)\subseteq\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{OC}}(X)\) be a finite typed
set of local OC requirements, and let \(\mathcal{B}^{\wedge}(X)\) be its
finite conjunction-closure. The allowed atom types are admissibility,
positive continuumness, operator-obligation satisfaction, identity
preservation, and falsifier satisfaction clauses. The notation
\(\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{OC}}(X)\) names only the finite requirement language
generated by those atom types for \(X\), and \(\mathcal{L}_{10}(\rho(X))\)
names the corresponding proposed \(K_{10}\) target language. The transport
\(\rho_{\ast,X}:\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{OC}}(X)\to\mathcal{L}_{10}(\rho(X))\)
maps each typed atom to its proposed \(K_{10}\)-language counterpart and
extends to finite conjunctions by preserving their typed clauses. Here
\(X\models r\) means satisfaction of that typed requirement under the
admissibility, continuumness, operator-obligation, identity, and falsifier
clauses of the surrounding proof route. An operator-obligation atom is satisfied
only when the relevant operator is defined on the declared source and target
objects and the mapped obligation preserves the operator's stated domain,
codomain, and admissibility guard; otherwise it fails. The expression
\(\rho(X)\models\rho_{\ast,X}(r)\) means satisfaction
of the translated requirement in the target language. The relation
\(\equiv_{\rho}\) is a typed compatibility relation on tested objects; if it
is used as a quotient, reflexivity, symmetry, and transitivity must be supplied
explicitly. In this section it is used only as a burden-preservation
compatibility test, not
as a decorative quotient. The reduction is lossless only if
\[
  \forall X\in\mathcal{U}_{11,12}\ \forall r\in\mathcal{B}^{\wedge}(X):
  \qquad X\models r \Longleftrightarrow \rho(X)\models \rho_{\ast,X}(r),
\]
and only if \(X\equiv_{\rho}Y\) never declares compatible two objects whose
admissible-state, continuumness, operator-obligation, identity, or falsifier burdens differ. This
makes the compatibility relation a preservation constraint rather than a
decorative quotient. In the reduction test, source-object compatibility and
source-to-target preservation are separate checks. The relation
\(\equiv_{\rho}\) constrains compatibility among source objects only; the
pair \(X,\rho(X)\) preserves identity only when the admissible-state,
continuumness, operator-obligation, identity-preservation, and falsifier
burdens of \(X\) match their translated \(K_{10}\) counterparts under
\(\rho_{\ast,X}\). Residue language
is only an analogy here, not the identity criterion for reduction.
The displayed condition is the local, formally quantified preservation test.
It is necessary for any reduction claim, but this worked example also records
a separate non-definitional coherence audit. For a finite family of tested
objects \(F\subseteq\mathcal{U}_{11,12}\), chosen requirements
\(r_X\in\mathcal{B}^{\wedge}(X)\), and a declared common meta-language
\(\mathcal{L}_F\), the audit must also declare translations
\(\iota_{F,X}:\mathcal{B}^{\wedge}(X)\to\mathcal{L}_F\). The family-level
test is the finite conjunction
\(\bigwedge_{X\in F}\iota_{F,X}(r_X)\) inside \(\mathcal{L}_F\), together
with any cross-object coherence clauses asserted for that family. In this
heuristic check, a
\(K_{10}\)-only reduction is flagged if it preserves every object locally
while losing a declared cross-framework coherence obligation carried by
\(K_{12}\). Thus, in the audit sense used here, ``reducible without loss''
would require preserving inside \(K_{10}\) alone all three burdens that the
release assigns to the upper levels:
\begin{enumerate}
\item explicit evolution of meta-theoretical objects as lawful structured
      states,
\item a distinction between local meta-model recursion and global
      cross-framework coherence conditions,
\item and the admissibility or collapse conditions attached to those
      higher-order objects.
\end{enumerate}
Accordingly, a critic would need to provide one explicit \(K_{10}\)-only
reduction map that preserves those burdens simultaneously. If such a map
exists, the upper levels should be demoted. If, however, the framework
genuinely needs one stratum for evolving meta-theoretical objects and another
for cross-framework coherence conditions, then the upper levels are not
optional. Core 1.3 takes the second position and, crucially, states the
preservation test under which that position could fail.
The role split is explicit: \(K_{11}\) carries the evolution of
meta-theoretical objects and operator/model spaces, while \(K_{12}\) carries
global cross-framework coherence, boundedness, and integrability conditions.
The same preservation criterion is used below whenever the table speaks of
numerical identity after collapse: identity is preserved only if the original
admissible-state and continuumness conditions remain live, not merely if a
later object can be related to residue.

\subsection{Interpretive Check 3: how the journal core is lawfully extracted from the monograph}

Readers often distrust large monographs because they have seen too many cases in which a short
article quietly says something stronger than the full technical work behind it. Core 1.3 is
designed to make that drift visible and prevent it.

The lawful extraction rule is asymmetric:
\[
\text{Master Monograph} \longrightarrow \text{Journal Core}.
\]
The journal core may compress, but it may not enlarge. That principle has several operational
consequences.

\begin{enumerate}
\item The journal core can suppress much of the didactic shell, but it must preserve the exact
      positive theorem scope.
\item It can omit broad projection discussions, but it may not promote any projection into
      accomplished proof.
\item It can shorten the literature screen, but it may not falsify the priority or comparator
      boundary set in the master.
\item It can move quickly through notation once the master has fixed it, but it may not invent a
      second notation universe.
\end{enumerate}

This is not an additional scientific premise. It is a release-governance rule for packaging and review. The
monograph is where the broader framework is taught, bounded, and audited. The journal core is
where one narrower result is exposed for fast hostile inspection. If the two diverge, the release
should be treated as broken.

\subsection{Why examples belong in a strict scientific monograph}

Examples are often treated as a concession to weaker readers. In foundational work that is a
mistake. A disciplined example does at least three scientific jobs at once. It tests whether the
formal grammar can be applied without hand-waving. It reveals where an apparent theorem is only an
interpretive gloss. And it gives editors and reviewers a compact way to see what would count as a
counterexample before they invest in line-by-line reconstruction.

That is why the present section is included inside the master monograph rather than hidden in an
outreach note. A large theory becomes more reliable when the examples are good enough to expose
its failure modes, not when the examples are removed in the name of austerity.
