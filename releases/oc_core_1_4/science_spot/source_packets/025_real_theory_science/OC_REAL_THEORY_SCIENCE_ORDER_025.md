# OC Real Theory Science Order 025

## Governing Rule

025 supersedes 024 as the proof/refutation authority for OC internal theory
claims. The 024 package remains useful as a boundary and source-audit artifact,
but no 024 status is proof-grade closure for 025.

Final scientific closure in 025 has exactly two statuses:

- `PROVED_WITH_PROOF`
- `REFUTED_WITH_COUNTERPROOF`

All other outcomes are attempt notes, boundary notes, or research state. They
must not appear as final ledger statuses.

## Mandatory Resume Procedure

Every resumed execution batch must:

1. read this order;
2. query `oc_real_theory_science_025.sqlite` for active claim and next action;
3. open `OC_REAL_THEORY_LAB_NOTEBOOK_025.md`;
4. continue the next proof/refutation attempt before writing any closeout.

If a runtime boundary interrupts the campaign, record a forced handoff in the
DB and notebook. A handoff is not completion.

## Scientific Scope

The 025 campaign concerns OC's own model before external applications:

- central principle;
- primitive definitions;
- operator catalogue and minimality;
- K-level hierarchy and count;
- theorem and law candidates;
- crown-stone claims;
- domain projections.

External Hilbert/open-problem work is application/frontier research and cannot
serve as foundation proof for OC itself.

## Proof Standard

Every terminal claim must include:

- exact source-bound statement;
- proposition package with hypotheses and conclusion;
- falsifier or countermodel criterion;
- dependency list;
- proof body or counterproof body;
- independent verification note;
- artifact reference.

An axiom is not a proof. If a source claim says a principle is proved but the
best support is only axiomatic adoption, then the claim "proved as theorem/law"
must be refuted and the remaining conditional consequences must be proved
separately.

## Current First Front

The first formal front is the archive filing example from
`022/public_payload/chapter_sources/master_monograph/chapter_Candidate_Laws_and_Scope_Conditions.md`.

The task is to prove or refute the local proposition:

> A whole-document binary public/private classification cannot satisfy a mixed
> document that must disclose one part and withhold another part, while an
> enriched classification with a part-level release/redaction axis can satisfy

This local result can support a bounded OC theorem. It does not prove a
universal law across all domains.
