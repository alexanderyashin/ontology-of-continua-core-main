# Manifest And Checksums
## The Local Problem In Manifest Checksums
This appendix names two plain instruments that help a large scientific book remain readable after its parts have multiplied. A public record is a list of the materials included in a given public edition: chapters, appendices, figures, tables, source files, bibliography records, and other named objects. A recorded digest is a short computed identifier for the exact contents of a file. If a single character changes, the identifier changes.
The authority of these instruments is deliberately narrow. They do not prove OC, validate an argument, certify a simulation, or make a citation stronger than it is. They answer a simpler question: what exact object was present when a claim, figure, table, appendix, or file was being discussed?
## The Necessary Distinction In Manifest Checksums
OC uses several terms across the book, and a reader can have them in view before the checking habit begins. A continuum is a described field of variation. A boundary marks a change in admissible description or interaction. An operator is an action or transformation that changes what can be said about a system. A projection is a disciplined translation of OC vocabulary into a domain that has its own observables and limits.
An inventory fixes where such terms are carried. It records the existence and identity of public-facing objects: chapter sources, appendices, figures, tables, equations, proof-facing material where present, notebook outputs where included, and bibliography entries. It does not turn a missing proof into a proof, a model comparison into an empirical result, or an illustrative example into a dataset. It fixes the object of inspection so that disagreement can attach to something definite.
## The Testable Step In Manifest Checksums
Inspection begins with identity, not agreement. The first question is whether the named object exists in the public edition. The second is whether its recorded digest matches the recorded identifier. The third is whether the surrounding prose uses the object within its declared status: citation as background, example as illustration, formal statement as formal statement, and evidence only where evidence is actually present.
A miniature case shows the point. Suppose one version of a chapter contains a figure comparing two boundary diagrams, and a later sentence argues from the contrast between them. If the figure file is silently replaced, the sentence may appear unchanged while its object has altered. A recorded digest would catch the changed file. It would not decide whether the new or old diagram is scientifically better. It would simply prevent the reader from unknowingly arguing about one figure while looking at another.
The same applies to a bibliography entry. If a citation to Kadanoff or Wilson is revised from a general background reference to a more specific source, the public record can show which bibliography object belongs to the edition being read. It cannot grant OC the authority of scaling theory or renormalization physics. It only keeps the citation from drifting while the argument is examined.
## An Objection Worth Keeping In Manifest Checksums
Cross references are connective rather than decorative. If a later projection borrows the language of boundary formation, the reader needs a route back to the formal vocabulary and forward to the domain-specific limits. If a passage compares OC’s scale language with Kadanoff’s block-spin picture or Wilson’s renormalization program, the reader needs to know where the comparison is made and where it stops.
The point is not to collect famous names around OC. It is to prevent a familiar scientific vocabulary from doing work it has not earned in this book. Kadanoff and Wilson may orient the reader toward scale-dependent description, but their presence does not make an OC claim true. Prigogine may orient a discussion of dissipative organization, but the citation does not imply that OC has reproduced his results. Landau and Lifshitz may supply a background in which microscopic and macroscopic descriptions are related with care, but that background cannot substitute for OC’s own arguments.
A public record helps preserve these distinctions by keeping references, figures, formal passages, and examples attached to their named objects. The reader can then ask a sharper question: does the passage use the borrowed vocabulary as orientation, analogy, formal support, or evidence? Those are different acts, and the book should not let them blur.
## The Result Of This Passage In Manifest Checksums
The central limit can be stated once: identity control is not truth control. A public record can show that a file belongs to the edition. A recorded digest can show that the file is exactly the file named. Neither can show that an argument is sound, that a formalization is complete, that a dataset is adequate, or that a simulation captures the relevant world. Those burdens remain with the chapters, appendices, proofs, data descriptions, comparisons, and objections that bear them.
Absence remains absence. If a scientific claim requires evidence that is not present, a list of nearby materials cannot supply it. If a projection is illustrative, it remains illustrative. If a formal file is partial, it remains partial. If a comparison has not been performed, no inventory entry turns it into a completed comparison.
The objection is easy to understand: why spend attention on lists and computed identifiers when the theory itself is the matter? Because large arguments often weaken in quieter ways than direct falsehood. Examples migrate. Versions blur. A citation begins as orientation and is later heard as evidence. A figure changes while the paragraph that depends on it stays the same. public record and recorded digest discipline restrains those ordinary failures of reading.

The reader’s practice is therefore modest and useful. When a later chapter leans on an object, ask whether the object is named, whether its identity can be fixed, and whether the text is using it at the right scientific strength. A figure may clarify without proving. A citation may orient without transferring authority. A formal passage may define a relation without showing that the relation governs the world.
If those distinctions remain visible, the appendix has done its public work. It has not settled ontology, physics, mathematics, evidence, or interpretation. It has made the material stable enough for criticism to reach the argument itself.
