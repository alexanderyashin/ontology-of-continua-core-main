# Title Page
## Invitation and Scope In Title Page
OC Core 1.4 is a scientific monograph about a proposed operational language for describing systems whose behavior cannot be captured by a single preferred vocabulary. Its subject is the Ontology of Continua, abbreviated here as OC: a way of speaking about systems, boundaries, continua, operators, contradiction, emergence, collapse, and projection across domains. A first encounter with the book begins before any formal claim appears. The title page must therefore do one quiet but decisive thing: identify the work without asking the first-time reader to accept the theory in advance.
The promise is limited and exact. The book will not assume that the reader already knows ontology, formal proof, Lean, percolation theory, scaling theory, or the internal history of the project. It will begin from the need for a language that can compare systems while preserving the fact that different domains expose different observables. A physical lattice, a social institution, a biological boundary, and a mathematical structure do not offer the same measurements. OC does not erase that difference. It asks whether a common descriptive grammar can state where comparison is possible and where it fails.
## What the Reader Can Test In Title Page
A title page is not an abstract, a theorem, or an evidentiary exhibit. It names the object being entered. For OC Core 1.4, the page carries the public title, the version, the artifact kind, and the subject area in a form stable enough for later citation. The title must not imply that the theory has already been proven, experimentally confirmed, mechanized, or accepted by comparison with rival frameworks. It marks a monograph, not a verdict.
The concrete example is simple. Suppose a later chapter discusses percolation, the study of connectivity and cluster formation in random media, with reference to standard treatments such as Stauffer and Aharony's account of percolation clusters and Grimmett's mathematical development of the subject. The title page must not make that future discussion look like an endorsement from percolation theory. It only prepares the bibliographic and conceptual entry point from which such comparisons can later be introduced, bounded, and evaluated.
## Bibliographic Position In Title Page
The required bibliographic matter is spare: public title, version, author or responsible authorship line where the edition supplies one, artifact kind, date or edition marker, and any publisher or repository designation used by the released volume. These elements allow the work to be referred to without importing internal machinery into the public text. A reader encountering a citation to OC Core 1.4 must be able to identify the document before being asked to evaluate any of its arguments.
This discipline matters because later chapters will move between ordinary explanation and technical neighborhoods. When the book introduces continua, it will use the word in its own operational sense: a structured range of possible states or descriptions along which a system can vary. When it introduces an operator, it will mean a rule or transformation that acts on such structured descriptions. When it introduces collapse, it will mean the failure of a configuration to preserve a viable organization under unresolved contradiction. None of those terms belongs on the title page as unexplained machinery. The page opens the door; the chapters earn the vocabulary.
## Scientific Temper In Title Page
The title page may include a restrained navigation cue, but only one that helps the reader understand the kind of work ahead. OC Core 1.4 proceeds from motivation into vocabulary, then into hierarchy, mathematization, evidence, projection, comparison, limits, reproducibility, appendices, and bibliography. That order is part of the intellectual contract. The book asks the reader first to understand the problem: how to state cross-domain claims without pretending that all domains share the same observables.
A useful cue can say that the work is an operational metaontology. The term metaontology means a study of how ontological descriptions themselves are formed, compared, constrained, and revised. In OC, the adjective operational is important: descriptions are not treated as decorative labels, but as objects that must support stated distinctions, transformations, and possible rejection. The title page can identify that ambition, while leaving the actual argument to the body of the monograph.
## Route Through the Book In Title Page
The public title page must be free of internal metadata. It must not display construction notes, process labels, file identifiers, validation handles, private routing terms, or evidence of manuscript assembly. Such matter may be necessary in the making of a book, but it is not part of the book's public argument. A monograph gains force by presenting claims, definitions, examples, objections, and limits in the language of the field, not by exposing the machinery that prepared the page.
This absence is not concealment of scientific weakness. It is genre discipline. Later chapters may cite Goldenfeld's lectures when discussing scaling and renormalization as ways of thinking about structure across levels, or Kadanoff when introducing the conceptual force of scaling arguments. Those citations belong where their ideas are explained and used. The title page is too early for that burden. It must not create an aura of rigor by name-dropping future authorities. It must simply identify the work so the later uses of those authorities can be judged in place.

The page succeeds when a first-time reader can answer four plain questions before turning forward. What is this work called? What kind of work is it? What subject does it address? What level of commitment is being asked at the beginning? The answer to the last question must be modest: only enough commitment to read. No theorem has yet been shown, no dataset inspected, no simulation replayed, no formalization evaluated, and no competing account displaced.
An objection naturally arises: if the title page is so restrained, can it carry the weight of a scientific monograph? Yes, because its task is not to argue but to prevent the wrong argument from starting. A title page that overclaims forces later chapters to repair trust before they can teach. A title page that under-identifies the work leaves the reader without a stable object. OC Core 1.4 needs the middle path: exact naming, clear genre, disciplined status, and no premature demand for belief. From there the introduction can begin the real work, moving from the problem of cross-domain description to the first operational terms by which OC proposes to address it.
