# Audit Protocol
## The Local Problem In Audit Protocol
An audit protocol is a rule for reading a claim before agreeing with it. Here the phrase names a public discipline: every later scientific claim must say what it is about, what sort of claim it is, what would count as a check, and where the claim stops. Without that discipline, a theory of systems can become fluent too quickly. It can move from cells to economies, from networks to concepts, from collapse to emergence, while carrying the same words across domains as if the words had kept the same meaning.
The danger is not ambition. A theory that never travels beyond its first example may be tidy and useless. The danger is travel without declaration. Evidence, formal notation, simulation, analogy, and interpretation can all produce convincing sentences. They do not produce the same kind of warrant. A careful reader can be able to tell whether a sentence is reporting an observation, proposing a hypothesis, drawing a comparison, or working inside a formal system. Audit Protocol does not make a weak claim strong. It makes the weakness visible.
## The Necessary Distinction In Audit Protocol
Consider a forest after a long dry season. The ground is brittle. Needles have gathered under the trees. A strike lands near the edge of a stand. The first flame may go out in damp shade, burn a small patch of scrub, or move through connected fuel until the event becomes regional. The same scene can invite several languages at once.
A physicist might compare the spread to self-organized criticality, the family of models associated with Per Bak in which systems can settle near states where small disturbances sometimes have large consequences. A network theorist might describe the forest as a graph, in the broad mathematical language developed in modern network science, with nodes and links representing relations among parts. A biologist might ask about cycles of repair, reproduction, and dependency, recalling Stuart Kauffman's work on autocatalytic organization and later formal treatments of reflexively autocatalytic food-generated networks.
Each comparison may help. None automatically proves that the forest, the model, and the theory share one structure. A bad sentence would say: the fire proves that forests are self-organized critical systems. That sentence borrows authority it has not earned. A better audited sentence would say: this fire can be compared with criticality models in the limited respect that local ignition sometimes produces disproportionate spread, but the comparison is not a measurement of criticality unless the relevant variables, assumptions, and tests are supplied. The difference is not pedantry. It is the difference between inquiry and decoration.
## The Testable Step In Audit Protocol
Audit begins by separating claim type. A definition fixes vocabulary. A hypothesis proposes a relation that may fail. A theorem follows from stated assumptions inside a formal system. A measurement reports an observation made under a method. A simulation reports behavior of an implemented model. A comparison says that one structure resembles another in specified respects. A projection carries a description from one domain into another under declared limits, so that the translation can be inspected rather than merely admired.
OC Core uses the word continuum for a structured field of variation, not for a vague smooth background. A boundary is a condition that separates what is treated as inside a described system from what is treated as outside it. An operator is a repeatable transformation or action on the described structure. These terms become useful only when they remain local to the kind of claim being made. The operator in a formal derivation is not automatically the operator in a laboratory system, and neither is automatically the operator in a historical explanation.
This restraint matters most where the theory becomes most ambitious. Network science can compare social, biological, and technological systems because it abstracts relations into nodes and links; that abstraction is powerful because it also discards detail. Autocatalytic theory can illuminate circular production and mutual dependence, but an autocatalytic model is not the same thing as every system that feels self-maintaining. A simulation may show how an implemented model behaves, but it does not by itself show that nature behaves that way. The audit keeps the gain and the loss in view at the same time.
## An Objection Worth Keeping In Audit Protocol
An audited claim carries five things with it: object, status, warrant, boundary, and failure condition. The object is what the claim is about. The status says whether the sentence is a definition, hypothesis, theorem, observation, simulation result, comparison, projection, or interpretation. The warrant names the kind of reason currently offered for it. The boundary states the domain or assumptions under which the claim is being made. The failure condition says what would force narrowing, revision, or rejection.
The point can be read without symbols. If a later chapter says that a contradiction opens a new dimension of description or collapses an unresolved configuration, the audit asks what kind of statement that is at that point in the book. It may be a guiding hypothesis. It may later receive formal treatment under stated assumptions. It may be illustrated by examples. It may be compared with ideas from network science, autocatalytic organization, or criticality. These statuses are not interchangeable. A comparison to Bak's criticality does not become a measurement. A resemblance to RAF network structure does not become biological validation. A formal theorem, if present later, does not by itself certify that a chosen real-world case satisfies its assumptions.
The same discipline applies to absence. If no dataset is being used, the text must not sound as if data have spoken. If no external challenge result is being reported, the prose must not imply that such a challenge has been passed. If Lean or another proof assistant is mentioned later, its status must remain exactly what the available artifact supports. Audit Protocol is a grammar of honesty before it is a machinery of persuasion.
## The Result Of This Passage In Audit Protocol
A reader may worry that this slows the book down. Why not simply state the theory and let its power appear? The answer is that cross-domain theories fail most often at the joints. They do not usually fail because a single sentence is obviously absurd. They fail because words slide. Emergence becomes mystery. Collapse becomes drama. Network becomes any collection of things. Proof becomes plausibility. Analogy becomes evidence.
The audit is not a delay added to the theory. It is part of the theory's ability to remain readable under pressure. If the book later moves from a formal structure to a biological example, a reader can not have to guess whether the move is proof, illustration, analogy, or conjecture. If it moves from a model to a historical case, a reader can know what survived the translation and what was left behind.
There is still a limit. Audit Protocol cannot decide in advance which biological system is well described by an autocatalytic model, which social system is better represented as a network, or which physical process really belongs near the mathematics of criticality. It can only require that those moves be named and bounded. A protocol that pretended to settle every scientific question would become the very excess it was meant to prevent.
## The Local Problem In Audit Protocol protocol
The durable expectation is simple: later claims in OC Core are to be read by status and boundary, not by rhetorical force. Definitions define. Hypotheses risk failure. Formal results depend on assumptions. Simulations report model behavior. Measurements report observations under methods. Comparisons borrow structure without proving identity. Projections translate under constraints. Interpretations remain interpretations.
That expectation lets the book proceed without asking for premature belief. The theory can compare systems across domains while preserving the difference between a concept, a model, a proof, an example, and an empirical claim. The working vocabulary can now enter under pressure: continua, boundaries, operators, contradictions, emergence, collapse, and projection are not impressive words to be carried everywhere at once. They are auditable terms, useful only when their object, warrant, limit, and possible failure remain in view.
